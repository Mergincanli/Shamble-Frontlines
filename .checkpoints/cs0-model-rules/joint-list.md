- **Slice 1 needs all 20**: change-set 10's port drives every one (BP:270-272; 0003:797), on the stand-in and the model alike (BP:1059). No joint waits for a later change-set.
- **The coat has joints of its own.** GA13 turns each quarter by the thigh's angle on its side times a front or back gain (0.95, 0.75), adds a spring flare at speed and clamps it to -0.8..0.6 (G:168, 441-443). Fixed skin weights on hips and thighs give a fixed blend of joint turns, with no gain, spring or limit [inferred], so a weights-only coat would change how the coat moves: not the engine's call (section 6). The skirt (U 0.43 to 1.045, CB:30) hangs below the knee, since the boot that follows the lower leg tops at U 0.50 (CB:33) [inferred], so in Slice 1 it must move with the legs.
- **The spine joint stays.** GA7 splits the aim twist 0.45 spine, 0.55 chest (G:364-365); the flinch and the death tip use the split (G:438, 474); the correction's poses turn the chest 45 and 30 degrees against the hips (CR:28, 34, 36). One joint would put the whole turn at one pivot [inferred].
- **Left out**, as nothing in Slice 1 needs them: a root joint (section 2); a separate neck (G:366 drives one joint); toes (the toe-off roll is foot pitch at the ankle, G:359-360); twist joints (no GA row turns a limb along its length); helper, IK or hand-socket bones (BP:996; the fist point is data, section 4); hat and flap joints (they follow the head, CB:46-50); a gun joint (the gun is its own model without a skin: AD:17, GD:228-231).
- **Same work as legacy:** the 20 are the transforms legacy already moves, its 14 joints (C:454), the 2 ankles it adds at load (G:253-264) and its 4 coat panels (G:265). Bone upload: 20 x 64 B = 1,280 B per soldier per frame (64 B per bone: GD:321).
- **Rules for every joint:** lower case, digits, underscores; `_l` and `_r` are the soldier's own left and right (CB:11), and survive three.js's name cleaning where a Blender `.L` would not (AR:272-278). A joint is named after the segment it moves; its pivot (in Blender, the bone's head) is at the segment's upper end. The order in the skin is free: the engine maps names to bones once, at load. The engine reads lengths and rest frames from the bind pose, never from fixed numbers or legacy's hang-along-minus-Y assumption (G:250). Nothing here reaches the simulation: hits, hit shapes and shot starts stay simulation data (AP-1, 0005:1549-1555).

## 2. `root`: the node above the skeleton, not a joint
**Answer:** `root` is not a skin joint. It is the one node above both the skeleton and the mesh, which the game moves, turns and scales with one number (MG:44, CB:12); `hips` is the skeleton's top joint.
**Reason.** (a) Nothing in Slice 1 moves a joint at the origin: the root never tilts or sinks (GA14, LI:231), and legacy's root is the character group outside its joint map (C:454; `pelvis` has no parent, C:465), which the game only places and turns (G:2710). The position, facing and scale must move the skeleton and the mesh node together, so that the fixed padded bounding sphere (0003:306-312) moves with the soldier [inferred]; the node that carries them is a common parent of both, which a skin joint is not [inferred]. A root bone would therefore stay identity: a joint for later, and 64 B more per soldier per frame (GD:321). (b) graphics-engineer's draft reads `root` as the armature's top bone (GD:201-206), because bone names export as written (AR:223-224) while the armature object's export is unread (GD:249, 316-317). That matters only if something must find `root` by name in the file, and under this ruling nothing does: the engine finds the skeleton by `hips`, and the check finds non-joint nodes by structure. A root bone does not guard the unread case either: if the exporter made the armature object a joint, mark 3 would see an extra joint with or without it. So I disagree with that reading; the real export test (CP:148-150) settles how the armature is written.
**In glTF:** `skin.joints` holds exactly the 20 names, in any order; `hips` is the only joint whose parent is not a joint, so it is the skeleton root (a written `skin.skeleton` names `hips` or a node above it [inferred: spec section not re-read]). The mesh node and every non-joint node above `hips` (the armature's node, if the exporter writes one) carry an identity transform: the skin ignores them (spec:2242 via AR:183-186), but three.js culls by the mesh node's world matrix [inferred]. No node named `root` is required in the file.
**In the engine:** `root` is one client-owned node per soldier; it takes the position and facing from the snapshots and the one scale number, and the loaded file's top nodes hang under it. The stand-in builder makes the same node and hangs `hips` and its mesh on it, so the model replaces the stand-in with no engine change (BP:1059).
**In Blender:** the armature object is the one root (the brief's "one empty at the origin", CB:73, becomes it): at the origin, transforms applied, top bone `hips`, no root bone; the joined mesh (GD:224-227) is its child. Its name is free: it only names the skin (AR:223-224). An empty above it would add one identity node and pass the check, but is not needed.

## 4. Sockets on a gun, and how the hands meet them
**Names:** `socket_grip`, `socket_support`, `socket_muzzle`, `socket_sight`, `socket_eject` (MG:51; CR:43). Each gun's entry lists the sockets its file has, and the check asks for those (GD:207-213, 264-267): the rifle file has four (MG:48), the SMG five (MG:51). Every held gun lists grip, support and muzzle, because the two hands and the flash need them (CR:23; MG:50). Sockets are cosmetic: a shot's start is simulation data (CR:47; AP-1, 0005:1549-1555).
**Axes, one rule.** A socket is an empty node without a mesh. In glTF (the gun's frame: +Y up, +Z to the muzzle, +X the gun's left, origin at `socket_grip`: SB:11) a socket's local +Z is the direction it points, its local +Y its up at right angles to +Z, and +X completes a right-handed frame. Grip, support, muzzle and sight point along the bore with the gun's up: rotation identity, only their positions differ (`socket_grip` at the origin: SB:11; CP:47-50). `socket_eject` points where the case flies: for the SMG, "right and 20 degrees up" (SB:40) gives +Z = (-0.940, 0.342, 0) and +Y = (0.342, 0.940, 0) in the gun's axes [computed: cos 20° = 0.940, sin 20° = 0.342].
**In Blender** (the gun modelled Z up, muzzle toward -Y, its left +X: SB:14) the exporter turns Blender -Y into glTF +Z and +Z into +Y: confirmed for locations (AR:197), inferred for rotations (GD:119-121). So in Blender a socket's direction is its local -Y and its up its local +Z: the grip, support, muzzle and sight empties keep identity rotation, and the SMG's eject empty is turned until its -Y points along (-0.940, 0, 0.342) and its +Z along (0.342, 0, 0.940). Because the rotation step is inferred, a real Blender export of an empty turned 5 degrees must read back within 0.5 degrees (mark 7's planted defect, CP:136-146) before Blender files rely on this rule; the script-written rifle file (CP:148-150) cannot prove it.
**How the hands meet the gun.** The rule is the correction's and Mustafa's: a pose sets where `socket_grip` sits, which way the gun points and how far the chest turns; the right hand is on `socket_grip`, the left on `socket_support`; the butt and the muzzle follow from the gun's file (CR:23; MG:49). The pose numbers are change-set 10's.
- Right hand: `upper_arm_r` → `lower_arm_r` → `hand_r`, onto `socket_grip`. Left hand: `upper_arm_l` → `lower_arm_l` → `hand_l`, onto `socket_support` (SB:42), one point in place of legacy's rail slide (CC9, LI:139). Legacy solves both the same way (G:415-416).
- The fist, not the wrist, meets the socket (GA3, LI:220; G:243): the hand's point lies `palm` metres beyond the hand joint (the wrist) on the lower arm's line, so the IK's second bone is lower arm + palm (G:415). `palm` is one number per soldier for both hands (legacy measures one, G:247), kept in the soldier model's entry, not as a bone (BP:996); the check compares it with the centre of the vertices weighted to `hand_r`, within 1 cm, GA8's own hand target (LI:225) [proposal]. The brief's hand centre lies on the forearm's line 0.057 m beyond the wrist [computed from CB:36, 40], so the rule fits the brief; the correction's reach, "0.62 from the shoulder joint to the middle of the hand" (CR:23), is upper arm + lower arm + palm, 0.636 for the brief's arm (CR:13).
- The hand's turn: `hand_*` keeps its rest turn in the hold, as in legacy (G:413), unless change-set 10 aligns it to the socket; under route (b) `hand_r` carries the gun, so change-set 10 sets its turn from the gun (GD:290-295). The list is the same either way.
- What carries the gun: in legacy's default soldier, `chest` (C:453, 697), so breathing, recoil and the flinch move gun and hands together (GA7, GA12: LI:224, 229); the correction's poses, given in the soldier's frame, convert into the chest's frame at their own chest turn [inferred]. Change-set 10 may choose another joint of the list.
- Change-set 10's pose needs and the joints that carry them: the chest turned against the hips by 45 and 30 degrees with the head facing forward (CR:28, 34, 36): `spine` + `chest`, with `head` turning back; the body leaning 10 degrees forward (CR:30, 38): `spine` (legacy's lean, G:364), or `hips` if the whole body leans; the left hand reaching `socket_support`: the left arm chain plus `palm`.

## 5. The soldier's origin and facing: a check a file passes or fails
Read in the file's bind pose; the file passes only if every line holds (axes: CB:11; CP:51-53; spec:732 via AR:160-161).
1. Upright: `head` above `chest` above `hips` above both feet, by joint height (glTF y). A Z-up file read as Y-up fails.
2. Front +Z: for each foot, the centre of the vertices weighted to `foot_*` lies ahead of the foot joint (larger z): the toes point forward (CB:34: heel F -0.08, toe F 0.20).
3. Left +X: every `_l` joint has x > 0 and every `_r` joint x < 0. With line 2 this catches a file turned round (both fail) and a mirrored one (line 3 fails).
4. Origin on the ground between the feet: the lowest vertex at y = 0 within 1 mm [proposal]; the mid-point of the two foot joints at x = 0 within 1 cm [proposal]; z = 0 lies between the heel's and the toe's z.
5. One root: `hips` is the only joint without a joint parent; the mesh node and every non-joint node above `hips` are identity, and each joint's rest transform equals its bind pose (from the inverse bind matrices), within 0.5 degrees (CP:136-146) and 1 mm [proposal], so the stand-in and the model hand the engine the same lengths and frames.
Planted files that must fail [proposal]: turned 180 degrees, mirrored, Z-up, origin moved 1 m; the unweighted vertex is mark 3's planted defect (CP:64).

## 7. What this list changes for change-set 10
- **Size:** smaller and more direct. The 20 joints are the 20 transforms legacy already moves, so change-set 10 ports GA1-GA15 one to one by name (legacy `pelvis` → `hips`, `neck` → `head`, `hipL` → `upper_leg_l`, `kneeL` → `lower_leg_l`, `shL` → `upper_arm_l`, `elbowL` → `lower_arm_l`, `handL` → `hand_l`, the load-time ankle → `foot_l`, the same on the right, `gc_skFL` → `coat_front_l` and so on), and drops legacy's two load-time fix-ups (the ankle re-hang, G:253-264; the palm measure, G:243-247, now data): no knee, ankle or coat to rebuild.
- **Risk:** three port traps, each tested on the stand-in. Legacy's sides look mirrored (its `R` joints and its gun at +X while it faces +Z: C:453, 492; G:2710) [inferred], so its X-signed numbers and turn signs flip once, in one place. World-to-soldier sums must include the root's scale (legacy uses the facing turn only, G:357).
